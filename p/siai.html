Report: Leveraging Locally Deployed and Fine-Tuned AI for Enhanced Clinical and Service Applications at Siriraj Hospital

II. Introduction: AI Transformation Opportunity at Siriraj Hospital

Siriraj's Profile: Scale, Role, and Technological Readiness

Siriraj Hospital stands as a cornerstone of Thailand's healthcare system. It is the nation's largest public hospital, situated on a sprawling 40-acre campus, managing an immense patient load with over three million outpatient visits and approximately 85,000 inpatient admissions annually.1 With a capacity exceeding 2,000 beds and a staff of around 1,000 specialist physicians, its scale of operation is among the largest in Southeast Asia.1 Beyond its size, Siriraj serves as a crucial final referral center, receiving patients with complex and rare diseases from across Thailand, solidifying its reputation as a trusted institution for advanced medical care.1 This dual role as a high-volume provider and a specialist center generates a vast and diverse dataset, encompassing both common ailments prevalent in the Thai population and unique, complex cases requiring specialized expertise.

This extensive operational scale and clinical complexity inherently present significant challenges, particularly concerning efficiency, resource allocation, and managing patient flow, especially given that specialty departments often operate at full capacity with long waiting lists.5 However, Siriraj has demonstrated a consistent commitment to adopting advanced technology to address these challenges and enhance care. The hospital is equipped with cutting-edge medical devices, including an MRI-Linear Accelerator for improved radiation therapy visualization and 4th Generation da Vinci surgical robots.1 Its diagnostic capabilities are extensive, featuring comprehensive imaging modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, PET-CT, ultrasound, fluoroscopy) supported by a Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS), advanced laboratory diagnostics processing over 23,000 tests daily, state-of-the-art microbiology labs, extensive genetic testing capabilities including Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) for cancer mutations, and digital pathology services.3 The presence of electronic health records (EHR) is implied by studies analyzing large numbers of records and the need for data integration for predictive modeling.5

Critically, Siriraj has made strategic investments in foundational digital infrastructure, positioning it for significant AI adoption. A key partnership with Huawei established a comprehensive 5-year framework to transform Siriraj into a '5G Smart Hospital'.13 This project implemented a Mobile Edge Computing (MEC)-based 5G private network, a hybrid cloud infrastructure integrating hospital departments, and a combined 5G + Wi-Fi 6 solution.13 This infrastructure is designed explicitly to support new medical applications requiring high bandwidth, low latency, and enhanced security, such as AI-assisted diagnostics and remote care.13 Furthermore, Siriraj was the first hospital in Southeast Asia to deploy NVIDIA DGX A100 systems, powerful AI supercomputers suited for demanding medical research and clinical applications.14 Complementing this hardware is the Siriraj Innovation Lab, established to foster collaboration between hospital personnel, external partners, and health tech companies to develop medical innovations.17

These infrastructure investments are not merely theoretical; Siriraj has already initiated several AI projects. An AI system assists radiologists in analyzing chest X-rays, reportedly achieving 91.35% accuracy and now utilized in over 40 Ministry of Public Health (MoPH) hospitals, demonstrating successful development and dissemination.3 Other projects include using the YOLO (You Only Look Once) model for automated centroblast cell counting in pathology slides for lymphoma grading 19, AI screening for diabetic retinopathy 19, AI-assisted voice and face recognition for mental health assessment 19, the SiCar AI Lab collaboration with CARIVA focusing on medical AI model development 20, AI-powered pathology information systems at the affiliated Siriraj Piyamaharajkarun Hospital (SiPH) 8, AI research via the SiData+ center 21, and AI applications targeting cancer biomarkers.22 This portfolio of existing projects, coupled with the advanced infrastructure, signals strong institutional readiness and a strategic vision for AI-driven healthcare transformation. The scale and coordination of these investments suggest a deliberate, top-down strategy aimed at establishing Siriraj as a leader in medical AI, potentially extending its influence regionally.1

The Case for Local AI Deployment in the Thai Healthcare Context

Deploying AI models locally—within Siriraj's own infrastructure—offers compelling advantages within the specific context of the Thai healthcare system. Thailand faces a significant burden from non-communicable diseases (NCDs), which account for the majority of deaths.24 Stroke, ischaemic heart disease, kidney diseases, liver cancer, diabetes, and various cancers (lung, breast) are leading causes of mortality.24 Communicable diseases like lower respiratory infections, tuberculosis (TB), and periodically dengue fever and influenza also remain important public health concerns.24 Locally deployed AI can be specifically fine-tuned and applied to improve the diagnosis, prediction, treatment planning, and management of these conditions most relevant to Siriraj's patient population.

Furthermore, healthcare systems in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), including Thailand, often face challenges such as resource constraints (financial, human, technical), data sharing difficulties, and varying levels of digital infrastructure and literacy.29 Data fragmentation, lack of standardization, interoperability issues between systems, concerns about data privacy and security, and sometimes a lack of motivation or awareness regarding data value can hinder the development and deployment of AI solutions that rely on large, diverse datasets.29 While Siriraj appears more technologically advanced than many institutions, some of these challenges, particularly around data standardization and sharing protocols, likely persist.29 Local deployment provides greater control over the hospital's data assets, potentially reducing reliance on external cloud providers and associated long-term costs. It allows Siriraj to build AI solutions tailored precisely to its environment, leveraging its unique data while navigating privacy regulations more directly.

Siriraj's specific operational context—extreme patient volume leading to capacity constraints and long waiting lists 5—further strengthens the case for local AI. AI applications focused on optimizing internal workflows, improving diagnostic efficiency, automating administrative tasks, and allocating resources more effectively can directly address these critical bottlenecks. Local deployment ensures that these optimization tools can operate with low latency and integrate tightly with existing hospital systems, maximizing their impact on operational efficiency.

Report Objectives and Structure

This report aims to provide a comprehensive analysis and strategic roadmap for the Data Scientist leading AI initiatives at Siriraj Hospital. It focuses specifically on the potential of locally deployed and fine-tuned AI models, including Large Language Models (LLMs) like Gemma, medical imaging frameworks like MONAI and models like TotalSegmentator, and predictive analytics tools. The analysis covers:

  1. Identifying high-value clinical and service applications within Siriraj.
  2. Assessing the capabilities and fine-tuning requirements of specific AI models (Gemma, MONAI, TotalSegmentator) for the Thai context.
  3. Detailing strategies and requirements for local deployment, emphasizing compliance with Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), data security, latency, and IT integration.
  4. Identifying other relevant AI models suitable for local implementation.
  5. Synthesizing how these models can be integrated into clinical pathways.
  6. Evaluating the technical challenges, resource needs, and validation processes from a data science perspective.

The following sections will delve into each of these areas, providing detailed analysis and actionable considerations to guide Siriraj's local AI strategy.

III. Identifying High-Impact AI Use Cases within Siriraj Workflows

Given Siriraj's scale, referral status, and existing technological base, numerous clinical and administrative workflows present opportunities for significant improvement through the strategic application of locally deployed AI. Prioritizing these opportunities requires considering potential impact, feasibility, and alignment with the hospital's mission.

Analysis of Clinical Workflows

  • Diagnostics: This is a primary area where AI can augment the capabilities of Siriraj's clinicians. Building on the success of the AI-assisted chest X-ray analysis 3 and the AI-powered pathology initiatives 8, further applications can be developed. AI models, potentially leveraging frameworks like MONAI, can be trained or fine-tuned on Siriraj's vast imaging data (CT, MRI, PET-CT, digital pathology slides) 3 to assist radiologists and pathologists in the faster and more accurate detection, segmentation, and characterization of findings related to prevalent Thai conditions such as liver cancer 24, lung cancer and TB 24, stroke 16, cardiovascular diseases 24, and breast cancer.24 AI can also aid in analyzing complex genomic data for targeted cancer therapies.3 The goal is not necessarily replacement but augmentation – improving speed, consistency, and potentially detecting subtle signs missed by the human eye under high workload pressure.
  • Documentation: The sheer volume of patients at Siriraj 1 inevitably places a heavy documentation burden on clinicians. Locally deployed and fine-tuned LLMs, such as Gemma adapted for Thai medical language, offer a significant opportunity to alleviate this burden. These models could assist in drafting admission notes, progress reports, procedure summaries, and discharge summaries directly within the EHR, potentially pulling structured data and allowing clinicians to edit and finalize, saving considerable time [Query Point 2a]. Summarizing lengthy patient encounters or previous records could also expedite clinical review.
  • Treatment Planning: AI, particularly in medical imaging, can enhance treatment planning precision. Automated segmentation tools (like TotalSegmentator or custom MONAI models) can delineate tumors and organs-at-risk with high accuracy and consistency, crucial for radiotherapy planning, especially with advanced equipment like the MRI-Linac.1 AI could also potentially predict treatment response based on imaging features or integrated clinical and genomic data 3, supporting personalized medicine approaches.
  • Patient Monitoring: For inpatients, particularly those in high-acuity settings or general wards, AI models can provide early warnings of clinical deterioration. By analyzing real-time streams of vital signs and laboratory results from the EMR 11, predictive models can identify patients at risk of events like cardiac arrest or requiring ICU transfer before overt signs become apparent.11 This allows for earlier intervention by rapid response teams, potentially improving outcomes and optimizing the use of critical care resources.

Analysis of Service and Administrative Workflows

  • Scheduling Optimization: The long waiting lists and full capacity operations reported at Siriraj 5 highlight a critical need for optimized scheduling. AI-driven predictive models, trained on historical appointment data, patient flow patterns, and resource availability (beds, operating rooms, specific equipment like the MRI-Linac 1), could forecast demand and optimize scheduling templates. This aims to reduce patient wait times, improve resource utilization, and enhance overall throughput, similar to initiatives at other large hospitals.38
  • Billing and Compliance: Medical billing in Thailand involves specific regulations and coding systems (e.g., ICD-10 39). AI, particularly NLP models, could assist by automatically extracting relevant diagnostic and procedural information from clinical notes and suggesting appropriate billing codes, potentially summarizing information according to Thai regulatory requirements [Query Point 2c]. This could improve billing accuracy and speed up the revenue cycle.
  • Patient Communication & Education: Effective patient communication is vital for adherence and outcomes. Thai-language LLMs can be used to generate clear, concise, and culturally appropriate discharge summaries, medication instructions, and educational materials about common conditions (e.g., diabetes, hypertension) and treatments offered at Siriraj [Query Point 2b]. This supports Siriraj's mission of health promotion.1
  • Resource Management: Predictive analytics can extend to managing physical resources. For instance, AI models could forecast demand for pharmaceuticals and medical supplies based on patient volume, case mix, and historical consumption patterns, helping to optimize inventory levels and potentially reducing waste or stockouts, a future goal mentioned in relation to the 5G project.15

Prioritization Framework for AI Initiatives at Siriraj

Given the breadth of possibilities, a structured approach to prioritizing AI projects is essential for the data science lead. A potential framework could evaluate projects based on criteria such as:

  1. Clinical Impact: Potential to improve patient outcomes, enhance diagnostic accuracy, or improve patient safety.
  2. Operational Efficiency: Ability to reduce costs, save clinician/staff time, increase throughput, or alleviate specific bottlenecks like waiting lists.5
  3. Feasibility: Availability and quality of required data, technical complexity of model development and integration, estimated resource requirements (compute, personnel).
  4. Strategic Alignment: Contribution to Siriraj's core missions (clinical care, research, education 19, referral status 1) and alignment with existing strengths (e.g., imaging AI 7, 5G infrastructure 13).
  5. Scalability: Potential to scale the solution across departments or the entire hospital.
  6. Potential for Broader Impact: Likelihood of the solution being disseminable to other hospitals (following the chest X-ray AI example 3) or contributing to national healthcare goals.

Applying such a framework suggests that AI applications delivering significant efficiency gains may offer the highest initial value. The immense patient volume 1 means that even modest time savings per task (e.g., documentation, image review) or slight improvements in workflow (e.g., scheduling) can yield substantial aggregate benefits across the hospital, directly addressing the pressures of high demand and capacity constraints.5 Tools assisting with Thai language documentation or accelerating diagnostic imaging interpretation fall squarely into this category.

Furthermore, Siriraj's role as a premier teaching hospital, training hundreds of medical students annually 3, presents a distinct opportunity. AI tools developed and validated in-house can be integrated into the curriculum, providing trainees with exposure to cutting-edge technology, potentially using AI for assessment feedback, simulation exercises, or as learning aids for interpreting complex data. This creates a synergistic relationship where AI development benefits from trainee interaction and feedback, while medical education is enhanced by AI tools, fostering a future generation of clinicians comfortable with and knowledgeable about AI.

Finally, the successful deployment of Siriraj's chest X-ray AI to numerous other public hospitals 3 sets a powerful precedent. It demonstrates the institution's capability not just to develop AI but also to navigate the complexities of broader implementation. This experience establishes a potential pathway for future Siriraj-developed and validated AI tools to achieve national scale, positioning the hospital as a central hub for medical AI innovation, validation, and dissemination within Thailand, potentially influencing national standards and collaborating with regulatory bodies like the Thai FDA and NHSO.18

IV. Harnessing Locally Deployed LLMs: Gemma Fine-Tuned for Thai Medical Needs

Large Language Models (LLMs) represent a powerful class of AI with significant potential for transforming text-based workflows in healthcare. Google's Gemma models, offered with open weights, present a viable option for local deployment and customization within Siriraj Hospital.40

Gemma Model Overview and Suitability for Local Deployment

Gemma is a family of LLMs developed by Google, available in various sizes (e.g., 2 billion, 9 billion parameters).40 The open-weight nature means Siriraj can download and modify the models for its specific needs. Crucially, the smaller variants (like Gemma 2B or potentially Gemma 9B) are designed to be more computationally efficient than larger proprietary models, making local deployment feasible on existing or planned hospital infrastructure, such as Siriraj's NVIDIA DGX A100 systems 14 or potentially other enterprise-grade GPUs.43 Gemma models are compatible with standard AI development frameworks, including Keras, JAX, PyTorch, and Hugging Face Transformers, offering flexibility in the choice of tools for fine-tuning and deployment.40

Fine-tuning Gemma for Siriraj: Thai Language Data, Protocols, and Benchmarks

While pre-trained Gemma models possess multilingual capabilities 42, achieving optimal performance for specialized tasks within the Thai medical domain necessitates fine-tuning. This process adapts the model to the nuances of Thai medical terminology, language structure, and, critically, Siriraj-specific clinical protocols, documentation styles, and operational workflows [Query Point 2, Query Point 4].

The availability of Thai-specific medical language resources can significantly accelerate this process. Resources like the PyThaiNLP library provide foundational tools for Thai text processing.45 More directly relevant are Thai medical datasets collated on platforms like Hugging Face, such as pobpad, thai-med-pack, chatmed-5k-th, and HealthCareMagic-100k-th.46 Furthermore, the development of models like Eir Thai Medical LLM, an 8-billion parameter model specifically designed for Thai medical tasks and benchmarked against standard medical datasets (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, MMLU) 39, provides a valuable reference point, potential training data source, or even a base model for further fine-tuning. The existence of these resources demonstrates the feasibility of building high-performing Thai medical LLMs and offers benchmarks against which a fine-tuned Gemma model can be evaluated.

Evaluating Gemma's baseline performance in Thai is an important first step. Benchmarks like SeaExam and SeaBench, designed for Southeast Asian languages including Thai, show Gemma 2 9B performing competitively.48 Other multilingual benchmarks like M3Exam, XNLI, and Flores-200 can also provide insights.42 Comparing performance on these benchmarks and specific medical tasks (like those used for Eir 47) before and after fine-tuning will quantify the value added by local adaptation.

However, the most crucial data for fine-tuning will likely come from Siriraj itself. Curated datasets of anonymized or de-identified clinical notes, discharge summaries, pathology reports, administrative documents, billing records, and potentially patient communication logs, all reflecting actual practices at Siriraj, are needed to tailor the model effectively. This internal data captures the specific terminology, abbreviations, formatting conventions, and workflow patterns unique to the hospital.

Potential Applications

A locally fine-tuned Thai medical Gemma model could power several high-impact applications:

  • Clinical Documentation Assistance: Automating the initial drafting of various clinical documents (admission/progress notes, procedure reports, encounter summaries) in Thai, potentially integrating structured data fields from the EHR. This directly addresses clinician workload associated with high patient volume.1
  • Patient Communication Generation: Creating patient-friendly discharge instructions, summaries of conditions, explanations of treatment plans, and responses to frequently asked questions in clear, accessible Thai. This aligns with Siriraj's health promotion goals 1 and can improve patient understanding and engagement.
  • Administrative Task Automation: Assisting with administrative workflows such as summarizing clinical information for billing purposes according to Thai regulations and coding standards 39, optimizing appointment scheduling by interpreting free-text requests or notes, or intelligently routing patient inquiries submitted via portals or messaging systems.

Recommended Fine-tuning Strategies

Given the potential scale of data and the need for efficient use of computational resources, specific fine-tuning strategies are recommended:

  • Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT): Techniques like LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) are highly suitable.40 LoRA significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters by freezing the original model weights and introducing smaller, trainable "adapter" layers. This drastically lowers the computational cost and memory footprint of fine-tuning, making it feasible on existing GPU resources like the DGX A100s 14 without requiring massive clusters.43 It also simplifies deployment, as only the small LoRA weights need to be stored and loaded alongside the base model.41
  • Instruction Tuning: Beyond general domain adaptation, instruction tuning teaches the model to follow specific commands and generate outputs in desired formats.43 Training data would consist of pairs like {"instruction": "Summarize this patient encounter note for handover", "input": "", "output": ""}. This is crucial for tasks like generating structured reports or following specific Siriraj documentation templates.
  • Iterative Development: Start fine-tuning with smaller, high-quality datasets focused on priority tasks.41 Evaluate performance rigorously on Siriraj-specific validation sets. Iteratively expand the dataset, adjust hyperparameters (learning rate, LoRA rank), and potentially incorporate synthetic data generation 43 if real data is scarce for certain tasks, always validating the impact on performance and ensuring generated data reflects real-world scenarios.
  • Framework Selection: Utilize established frameworks that support Gemma and PEFT, such as KerasNLP with Keras 3 40, Hugging Face's transformers and peft libraries 41, or optimized libraries like Unsloth 43 known for memory efficiency. If leveraging Siriraj's hybrid cloud 13, Google Cloud tools for tuning and deployment might also be considered.44

Fine-tuning a model like Gemma on Siriraj's own data, reflecting its specific protocols and patient population, is fundamental for clinical acceptance. Generic models may produce outputs misaligned with local practices or terminology. A model demonstrably grounded in Siriraj's reality is far more likely to be trusted and adopted by clinicians, overcoming potential skepticism towards AI. This localization provides value beyond simple language translation; it embeds institutional knowledge.

However, a critical dependency arises from data privacy regulations. Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) imposes strict requirements on processing sensitive health data, typically requiring explicit consent from the data subject or another valid legal basis.49 Using patient records for fine-tuning AI models falls under this scope. Therefore, establishing a robust data governance framework, including processes for obtaining patient consent specifically for AI development or implementing rigorous anonymization/de-identification techniques that meet PDPA standards, is an essential prerequisite before large-scale fine-tuning using sensitive patient data can commence. This involves legal, ethical, and operational considerations that must be addressed upfront by the data science lead in collaboration with relevant hospital departments.

V. Advancing Medical Imaging with Local AI: MONAI and TotalSegmentator

Medical imaging is a cornerstone of modern diagnostics and treatment planning, and Siriraj Hospital's high volume of imaging studies 3 presents a prime opportunity for AI-driven enhancements. Frameworks like MONAI and powerful segmentation models like TotalSegmentator, when deployed locally, can significantly augment capabilities in this domain.

MONAI Framework and TotalSegmentator Capabilities

MONAI (Medical Open Network for AI) is an open-source framework built on PyTorch, specifically tailored for the unique challenges of medical imaging AI.30 It provides a comprehensive ecosystem encompassing:

  • MONAI Core: Offers domain-specific data loading, pre-processing transforms (crucial for handling diverse medical image formats and characteristics), network architectures, and evaluation metrics.54
  • MONAI Label: Facilitates AI-assisted annotation, accelerating the creation of labeled datasets needed for training and fine-tuning models.55
  • MONAI Deploy: Provides tools like the App SDK and MONAI Application Packages (MAPs) to standardize the packaging and deployment of medical imaging AI models into clinical environments, simplifying integration with systems like PACS.34 The MONAI Model Zoo serves as a repository of pre-trained models in the MONAI Bundle format, designed for reproducibility and ease of use, covering tasks like segmentation and classification, although specific models for all possible targets may require external sourcing or custom training.33

TotalSegmentator is a notable pre-trained deep learning model designed for comprehensive anatomical segmentation. It boasts the ability to segment up to 104 different structures in CT images and 56 structures in MRI images, covering a wide range of organs and tissues across the entire body.34 Pre-trained versions are available 57, and importantly, it can be integrated within the MONAI ecosystem, for example, packaged as a MAP using the MONAI Deploy tools, facilitating its deployment in a clinical workflow.34

Relevance to Siriraj's Imaging Needs and Thai Disease Prevalence

The capabilities of MONAI and TotalSegmentator align well with Siriraj's operational context and the health needs of the Thai population. Siriraj performs a vast number of imaging studies annually (over 655,000 patients served in 2023) across multiple advanced modalities, including CT, MRI, and PET-CT.3 AI tools that can automate or assist in the analysis of these images can significantly enhance efficiency and potentially accuracy.

Specific segmentation and detection tasks enabled by these tools can directly address diseases highly prevalent in Thailand 24:

  • Liver Cancer: Segmentation of the liver and detection/segmentation of tumors in CT or MRI scans.30
  • Lung Cancer & Tuberculosis: Detection and segmentation of lung nodules or TB-related lesions in CT or Chest X-ray (complementing existing AI 18).32
  • Stroke: Segmentation of brain structures and detection/quantification of ischemic or hemorrhagic lesions in CT or MRI.16
  • Cardiovascular Disease: Segmentation of heart chambers and major vessels from CT or MRI for functional assessment or planning.33
  • Breast Cancer: Detection or segmentation of suspicious lesions in mammography or MRI.32

Potential Applications

Locally deployed MONAI models and TotalSegmentator can be applied across the imaging lifecycle:

  • Automated Segmentation: This is a core strength. Applications include:
  • Segmenting organs-at-risk (OARs) automatically for radiation therapy planning, improving consistency and saving clinician time, particularly valuable with Siriraj's MRI-Linac.1
  • Quantifying tumor volumes or other anatomical structures to track disease progression or treatment response objectively.20
  • Providing anatomical context for surgical planning (e.g., defining liver segments before resection 30).
  • Using TotalSegmentator for broad, whole-body anatomical mapping, potentially followed by fine-tuned MONAI models for highly accurate delineation of specific targets like small tumors or complex lesions.
  • Radiology Decision Support: AI models built or fine-tuned using MONAI can assist radiologists by highlighting potential abnormalities, such as subtle lung nodules 33 or early-stage tumors, which might be challenging to detect consistently under high workload. This complements existing tools like the Chest X-ray AI.3
  • Treatment Planning Enhancement: Precise segmentations from MONAI/TotalSegmentator are directly applicable to defining target volumes and avoidance structures in radiotherapy.1 Longitudinal analysis of segmented tumor volumes can provide quantitative measures of treatment efficacy.32

Recommended Fine-tuning and Adaptation Strategies

To maximize the clinical utility and accuracy within Siriraj's specific environment, adapting pre-trained models or training new ones using local data is crucial:

  • Leverage MONAI for Fine-tuning: Utilize MONAI Core's capabilities to fine-tune pre-trained models (from the Model Zoo 33, TotalSegmentator 57, or other sources) on Siriraj's patient imaging data, accessed via PACS in DICOM format and potentially converted to NIfTI.3
  • Utilize Domain-Specific Transforms: Employ MONAI's rich library of data augmentation transforms designed specifically for medical images (e.g., rotations, scaling, intensity shifts, elastic deformations) to improve model robustness and generalization.30
  • Optimize Training Pipelines: Use MONAI's dataset caching mechanisms (e.g., CacheDataset, PersistentDataset) to store the results of computationally intensive, deterministic pre-processing steps (like loading and initial intensity normalization), significantly speeding up training epochs.58
  • Adapt to Local Data Characteristics: Fine-tune models to account for variations specific to Siriraj's imaging equipment, scanning protocols, and potentially characteristics of the Thai patient population. This ensures the model performs optimally on the data it will encounter in clinical practice.
  • Standardize Deployment with MONAI Deploy: Package validated and fine-tuned models using the MONAI Deploy SDK to create reproducible MONAI Application Packages (MAPs).55 This standard format simplifies deployment and integration into the hospital's infrastructure and workflows, as demonstrated by the TotalSegmentator-AIDE MAP.34

The availability of the MONAI Deploy framework 55 and the concept of standardized MAPs 34 represent a significant advantage. They provide a structured pathway to bridge the gap between a research-validated model and a clinically deployable application, addressing common challenges related to dependency management, reproducibility, and integration with hospital systems like PACS/DICOM. This allows the data science team to focus more on core model development and validation, reducing the burden of custom deployment engineering.

While a comprehensive model like TotalSegmentator offers impressive breadth 34, achieving the high level of accuracy often required for critical clinical decisions—such as precisely defining tumor boundaries for radiotherapy or detecting very subtle lesions—will likely necessitate further adaptation. Fine-tuning TotalSegmentator or developing specialized models using MONAI, trained extensively on Siriraj's own diverse patient data and imaging protocols, will be essential to capture local nuances and meet stringent performance requirements for specific tasks.30 Relying solely on off-the-shelf performance may not suffice for all intended high-stakes applications.

Furthermore, there is considerable potential in combining the outputs of imaging AI with LLMs. For example, quantitative metrics derived from MONAI/TotalSegmentator segmentations (e.g., tumor volume, organ measurements) could be automatically incorporated into draft radiology reports generated by a fine-tuned Thai medical LLM like Gemma. This multimodal approach could produce richer, more consistent, and potentially faster reports, leveraging Siriraj's strengths in both imaging 7 and potentially NLP AI.

VI. Expanding the AI Toolkit: Predictive Analytics and Other Models

Beyond LLMs and imaging AI, other classes of machine learning models, particularly predictive analytics, offer significant potential for local deployment at Siriraj to address clinical and operational challenges.

Predictive Models for Patient Outcomes and Resource Management

  • Patient Deterioration Prediction: A high-impact area involves developing models to predict the risk of clinical deterioration in hospitalized patients. Using routinely collected EMR data such as vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature) and laboratory values 11, machine learning algorithms (e.g., neural networks, gradient boosting) can be trained to identify patients at high risk of requiring ICU transfer or suffering a cardiac arrest.11 A key goal is to achieve higher positive predictive value compared to traditional rule-based Early Warning Scores (EWS), thereby reducing the high false alarm rates that contribute to alarm fatigue among clinical staff.11 Models incorporating longitudinal data (trajectories of vitals/labs over time) may offer superior performance, capturing dynamic changes in patient status.37 Implementing such a system requires robust integration with Siriraj's EMR to access near real-time data feeds. The success of these models, however, is critically dependent on the quality, completeness, timeliness, and accessibility of this EMR data. Gaps, delays, or inaccuracies in data capture, identified as potential challenges in LMIC settings 29, could severely undermine model performance and clinical utility. Close collaboration between the data science team, IT, and clinical informatics is essential to ensure a reliable data pipeline.
  • Resource Allocation Optimization: Siriraj's high patient volume and capacity constraints 5 make resource optimization a critical need. Predictive models can be developed to forecast demand for key resources like ICU beds, operating room time, or even staffing levels in specific units. By analyzing historical admission patterns, patient acuity, surgical schedules, and length-of-stay data, these models can help hospital administrators make more informed decisions about resource allocation, potentially smoothing workflows and reducing bottlenecks. However, the implementation of such models requires more than just technical accuracy; it necessitates integration into operational dashboards and decision-making processes. Gaining trust and buy-in from hospital management and clinical department heads is crucial, as acting on these predictions may require changes to established workflows and practices. Demonstrating the model's value proposition clearly and ensuring its outputs are presented in an actionable format are key to successful adoption.
  • Disease Management Support: Predictive analytics can also support the management of chronic diseases prevalent in Thailand.24 For example, models could predict the risk of complications in diabetic patients based on their clinical history and glycemic control, identify patients with cardiovascular disease at high risk of future events, or even forecast outbreaks of infectious diseases like influenza 26 or Dengue fever 27 based on hospital admission trends and potentially external data sources.

Other Potential AI Applications

  • Anomaly Detection: AI algorithms can be trained to identify unusual patterns or outliers in various data streams – clinical measurements, laboratory results, operational metrics, or even billing data. This can help flag potential data entry errors, detect emerging patient safety risks, identify rare disease presentations, or uncover opportunities for process improvement.
  • Research Support: Siriraj's role as a major research and teaching institution 19 can be significantly enhanced by AI. Models can automate the process of identifying patient cohorts meeting specific criteria for clinical studies directly from EHR data 36, analyze large-scale datasets to generate or test research hypotheses, or assist in matching patients to relevant clinical trials.
  • Genomic Data Analysis: With capabilities in NGS for cancer mutation detection 3, AI can play a role in analyzing complex genomic data, identifying clinically relevant mutations, predicting their impact, or correlating genomic features with treatment response and patient outcomes, furthering precision medicine initiatives.

VII. Framework for Local AI Implementation at Siriraj

Successfully implementing and sustaining locally deployed AI requires a comprehensive framework addressing data governance, technical infrastructure, regulatory compliance, and integration strategies.

Data Governance and Preparation for Fine-tuning

The foundation of any AI initiative is high-quality, accessible, and appropriately governed data. Siriraj must establish clear, robust policies and procedures specifically for AI development and deployment. This includes:

  • Regulatory Compliance: Policies must ensure strict adherence to Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) B.E. 2562 (2019) 49 and potentially the National Health Act B.E. 2550 (2007) 49 regarding the handling of sensitive health information. This involves defining lawful bases for processing (often requiring explicit patient consent for AI training on identifiable health data 50), procedures for managing data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure 49), and ensuring purpose limitation and data minimization principles are followed.51 International standards like HIPAA can provide useful benchmarks for best practices in security and privacy.59
  • Data Quality and Standardization: Addressing known challenges in healthcare data, such as inconsistency, incompleteness, and lack of standardization 29, is crucial. Efforts should focus on standardizing data formats, terminologies (e.g., using standard ontologies), and collection practices where possible. Siriraj's hybrid cloud infrastructure 13 could potentially host centralized data repositories or processing pipelines to aid standardization efforts.
  • Anonymization and De-identification: Implementing reliable techniques to remove or mask personally identifiable information (PII) and protected health information (PHI) is essential for using patient data in AI model training while complying with PDPA.60 The effectiveness of these techniques must be carefully validated to minimize re-identification risk.

Local Deployment: Technical Requirements and Strategic Benefits

Deploying AI models within Siriraj's own infrastructure offers significant advantages, particularly concerning regulatory compliance and data control, but requires careful planning.

  • PDPA Compliance Strategy: Local deployment inherently supports PDPA compliance by keeping sensitive patient data within Siriraj's controlled environment, simplifying oversight and reducing risks associated with cross-border data transfers, which are restricted under PDPA unless specific conditions are met.50 Key PDPA requirements to address include:
  • Lawful Basis: Primarily consent for sensitive health data.50
  • Data Subject Rights: Mechanisms for handling requests for access, correction, deletion, etc..49
  • Security Measures: Implementing appropriate technical and organizational security.51
  • Purpose Limitation & Minimization: Collecting and using only necessary data for defined purposes.51
  • Data Breach Notification: Reporting breaches to the PDPC within 72 hours.53
  • Data Protection Officer (DPO): Appointing a DPO, likely mandatory for Siriraj given its scale and data sensitivity.49
  • Record of Processing Activities (ROPA): Maintaining documentation of data processing.51 Local deployment directly addresses data residency concerns and facilitates easier auditing and control over these requirements compared to relying solely on external cloud providers. This strategic advantage in simplifying compliance for sensitive health data is a major driver for local deployment.
  • Robust Data Security Architecture: A multi-layered security approach is essential. This includes strong encryption for data both at rest and in transit 59, strict role-based access controls (RBAC) to limit data access to authorized personnel 59, network segmentation and security leveraging the capabilities of the 5G private network and MEC 13, secure environments for model training and inference, regular vulnerability scanning and penetration testing, and comprehensive audit logging.59 Infrastructure must meet necessary security standards.64
  • Ensuring Low Latency: For AI applications requiring near real-time responses (e.g., intra-operative guidance, real-time patient monitoring alerts), local deployment is critical. Siriraj's investment in 5G and MEC infrastructure is specifically designed to support such low-latency use cases by processing data closer to the source, avoiding delays associated with cloud communication.13
  • Integration with Siriraj's IT Ecosystem: Seamless integration is key to clinical adoption. AI models must interact effectively with existing core systems like the EHR, PACS 3, Laboratory Information Systems (LIS) 3, and potentially the newer 5G/Cloud platform.13 This requires careful planning of APIs, data exchange formats (e.g., leveraging standards like DICOM and FHIR where applicable 55), and workflow orchestration. Tools like the MONAI Deploy Informatics Gateway can facilitate integration with imaging systems.55 However, integrating AI across the diverse and potentially complex IT landscape of a large hospital like Siriraj, which involves multiple technology partners 13, remains a significant technical challenge requiring dedicated expertise and strong collaboration between the data science team, hospital IT, clinical informatics, and potentially external vendors. Siriraj's hybrid cloud setup 13 offers a degree of flexibility, potentially allowing compute-intensive training tasks to leverage scalable cloud resources (if data privacy can be maintained, e.g., through rigorous anonymization), while keeping sensitive data processing and real-time inference strictly localized.

Exploring Privacy-Enhancing Technologies (PETs)

While local deployment enhances control, additional PETs can further strengthen privacy, especially for future collaborations or sensitive internal data sharing:

  • Federated Learning (FL): This technique allows multiple parties (or departments within Siriraj) to collaboratively train a shared AI model without exchanging their raw local data.61 Each site trains the model on its local data, and only anonymized model updates (e.g., gradients or parameters) are aggregated centrally to improve the shared model. FL can significantly reduce privacy risks 65 and enable collaborations where direct data sharing is prohibited by policy or regulation. While potentially complex to implement due to communication overhead and device heterogeneity 65, it offers a powerful privacy-preserving alternative for certain scenarios.
  • Differential Privacy: This involves adding carefully calibrated statistical noise to data or model outputs to make it mathematically difficult (or impossible) to determine whether any specific individual's data was included in the dataset.61 While providing strong privacy guarantees, it often involves a trade-off with data utility or model accuracy, which needs careful evaluation for clinical applications.

VIII. Integrating AI into Clinical Pathways: A Synthesis

The true value of AI in healthcare is realized when it is seamlessly integrated into established clinical pathways, augmenting decision-making and improving efficiency at critical points. Standalone AI tools that require clinicians to deviate significantly from their normal workflow are less likely to be adopted, especially in a high-pressure environment like Siriraj.1

Illustrative Examples: AI-Enhanced Pathways

  • Acute Stroke Pathway:
  1. Patient Arrival (potentially via Mobile Stroke Unit 16): Initial assessment data entered into EHR.
  2. Imaging: Urgent non-contrast CT Brain scan performed.
  3. AI Integration: Locally deployed AI model (MONAI-based or custom, potentially building on planned CT Brain AI 18) analyzes CT images immediately upon acquisition via PACS integration.
  4. Alerting: If AI detects hemorrhage or signs of large vessel occlusion, an automated alert is sent via the EHR or secure messaging to the stroke team (neurologists, neurosurgeons, interventional radiologists).
  5. Decision Support: AI-generated segmentations or annotations are available in the PACS viewer alongside original images. A fine-tuned Gemma LLM could potentially generate a concise summary of the initial presentation and imaging findings for rapid review.
  6. Treatment: Faster diagnosis and characterization enable quicker decisions regarding thrombolysis or endovascular therapy.
  • Oncology Pathway (e.g., Liver Cancer Management):
  1. Diagnosis: Patient undergoes CT or MRI imaging. AI (TotalSegmentator for liver context, fine-tuned MONAI model for tumor detection/segmentation 30) assists radiologists by highlighting suspicious lesions and providing volumetric measurements, integrated into the PACS report. Pathology confirmation may involve AI analysis of digital slides.8
  2. Staging & Planning: AI-derived segmentations are used for accurate staging. If radiation therapy is planned, automated OAR segmentation using TotalSegmentator/MONAI 1 integrated with the planning system accelerates contouring. Genomic analysis 3 might be supported by AI.
  3. Patient Communication: A fine-tuned Gemma LLM generates patient-friendly explanations of the diagnosis and proposed treatment options in Thai, accessible via a patient portal or provided by the clinical team.
  4. Treatment Monitoring: Follow-up imaging is analyzed by AI to track changes in tumor volume quantitatively 32, providing objective measures of treatment response integrated into follow-up reports. Predictive models might forecast treatment response probability.
  • ICU/Ward Patient Monitoring Pathway:
  1. Data Collection: Vital signs and lab results are continuously fed from monitors and LIS into the EHR.
  2. AI Prediction: A locally deployed predictive deterioration model 11 analyzes incoming data in near real-time via EHR integration.
  3. Risk Stratification & Alerting: The model calculates a deterioration risk score. If the score exceeds a validated threshold, an alert is triggered within the EHR, potentially notifying the bedside nurse and/or a dedicated Rapid Response Team via secure messaging or pagers.
  4. Clinical Response: The alert prompts timely bedside assessment and potential intervention, aiming to prevent adverse events.
  5. Documentation Support: A fine-tuned Gemma LLM could assist in generating daily progress note summaries or standardized handover reports incorporating key events and risk scores.

Considerations for Workflow Redesign and Clinical Adoption

Implementing AI effectively often requires more than just adding a new tool; it necessitates rethinking and potentially redesigning existing clinical workflows. Key considerations include:

  • Seamless Integration: AI outputs must be presented within the clinician's primary tools (EHR, PACS) in an intuitive and actionable manner. Avoiding separate logins or manual data transfer is crucial for adoption.
  • Training and Guidelines: Clinicians need training on how to use the specific AI tools, understand their capabilities and limitations, and interpret their outputs correctly. Clear clinical guidelines should be developed outlining the role of the AI, when its output should be considered, and procedures for overriding AI suggestions when clinically appropriate.
  • Human Oversight: Especially for diagnostic and treatment decisions, AI should function as a supportive tool, not a replacement for clinical judgment. Mechanisms for human review and validation of AI findings must be embedded in the workflow.59
  • Feedback Mechanisms: Establishing channels for clinicians to provide feedback on AI performance, usability, and errors is vital for ongoing model improvement and refinement.
  • Impact Measurement: Define key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure the impact of AI integration on clinical outcomes (e.g., time to diagnosis/treatment, complication rates), operational efficiency (e.g., report turnaround time, length of stay, resource utilization), and staff/patient satisfaction.

Achieving successful integration requires demonstrating clear clinical utility and ensuring the safety and reliability of the AI tools within the specific context of Siriraj. Rigorous local validation using Siriraj's own diverse patient data is paramount.32 This process, potentially involving retrospective studies comparing AI performance against historical data and prospective studies evaluating real-world impact, is essential for building trust among clinicians and satisfying potential regulatory requirements from bodies like the Thai FDA 18, mirroring principles from international guidelines like those from the US FDA for AI/ML medical devices.59

IX. Technical Roadmap and Considerations for the Data Science Lead

Implementing a portfolio of locally deployed and fine-tuned AI models at Siriraj requires careful planning regarding technical challenges, resource allocation, validation, and ongoing maintenance.

Anticipating Technical Challenges

The data science lead must anticipate and proactively address several key technical hurdles:

  • Data Challenges: As highlighted previously, ensuring the availability, quality, consistency, and accessibility of data from diverse sources (EHR, PACS, LIS, genomics) is fundamental yet challenging.29 Addressing data heterogeneity, potential biases within the data reflecting historical practice or population demographics, and establishing robust governance compliant with PDPA 49 require significant, ongoing effort involving data engineering, clinical informatics, and legal/ethical oversight.
  • Validation Complexity: Moving beyond standard accuracy metrics (like Dice score for segmentation 32) to robustly validate clinical utility and safety is complex. This involves designing appropriate retrospective and prospective validation studies, defining clinically meaningful endpoints, assessing model performance across different patient subgroups to check for fairness and equity 59, monitoring for performance degradation (model drift) over time, and potentially comparing AI-assisted workflows against existing practices.
  • Integration Hurdles: Interfacing AI models with Siriraj's existing, potentially complex and varied IT infrastructure (including legacy systems) is a major technical undertaking.7 Developing stable APIs, ensuring data interoperability (e.g., handling different data formats and standards), and embedding AI outputs seamlessly into clinical workflows requires significant integration engineering effort and close collaboration with hospital IT teams and potentially system vendors.13
  • Scalability and Performance: Training large models (even with PEFT) and running inference for multiple AI applications concurrently demands substantial computational resources. Ensuring the local infrastructure (including the DGX A100s 14, network bandwidth, storage systems) can handle the load, managing GPU allocation efficiently 34, and optimizing models for inference speed are critical technical considerations.
  • Maintenance and Lifecycle Management: AI models are not static. They require ongoing monitoring for performance degradation due to changes in data distributions or clinical practices. Processes for periodic retraining, version control, deployment updates, and managing the entire model lifecycle (MLOps) are essential for long-term sustainability and reliability.

Resource Planning

Successful implementation demands adequate resources across several categories:

  • Computational Resources: Strategically utilize the existing NVIDIA DGX A100 systems 14 for demanding training and fine-tuning tasks. Evaluate the need for additional GPU capacity based on the planned portfolio of AI applications. Leverage the hybrid cloud infrastructure 13 judiciously, perhaps for less sensitive data processing or bursting compute needs, while ensuring compliance. Plan for significant data storage capacity for large imaging datasets, EHR extracts, and model artifacts.
  • Personnel: Building and sustaining a skilled, multidisciplinary team is arguably the most critical resource requirement. This team should include:
  • Data Scientists: Expertise in AI/ML algorithms, model development, and validation.
  • ML Engineers: Focus on building robust, scalable training and inference pipelines, MLOps, and deployment.
  • Data Engineers: Responsible for building and maintaining data pipelines, ensuring data quality, and managing data infrastructure.
  • Clinical Informaticians: Bridge the gap between clinical needs and technical solutions, assist with workflow integration and validation design.
  • IT Integration Specialists: Expertise in interfacing AI tools with existing hospital systems (EHR, PACS, LIS).
  • Data Protection Officer (DPO): Essential for ensuring PDPA compliance.49
  • Clinical Domain Experts: Close collaboration with radiologists, pathologists, oncologists, intensivists, nurses, etc., is non-negotiable for defining problems, labeling data, validating models, and ensuring clinical relevance. Siriraj can leverage existing expertise within units like SiData+ 21 and build upon collaborations (e.g., with CARIVA 20, Huawei 13, academic partners 19), but developing sustained internal capacity through hiring, training (potentially via the Innovation Lab 17), and retention strategies is crucial.
  • Financial Resources: Secure adequate budget allocation for hardware acquisition/maintenance, potential software licenses, personnel costs (salaries, training), data acquisition/curation efforts, clinical validation studies, and potential cloud usage costs associated with the hybrid infrastructure.

Validation, Monitoring, and Ensuring Clinical Utility

A rigorous approach to validation and monitoring is non-negotiable for deploying AI safely and effectively in a clinical setting:

  • Multi-Stage Validation Framework: Implement a framework that includes:
  • Technical Validation: Assessing model performance using appropriate metrics on held-out test datasets (accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, AUC, Dice coefficient, etc.). Evaluating robustness across different data sources or patient subgroups.
  • Clinical Validation: Evaluating the model's impact in simulated or real clinical settings. Does it change clinical decisions appropriately? Does it improve diagnostic accuracy or efficiency compared to standard practice? This often requires expert review or comparative studies.
  • Usability Testing: Assessing how easily clinicians can interact with the AI tool and integrate it into their workflow.
  • Continuous Monitoring: Implement automated systems to monitor the performance of deployed models in real-time or periodically. Track key metrics and set up alerts for significant performance degradation (model decay 60) or shifts in input data distributions (data drift).
  • Error Management: Establish clear processes for clinicians to report perceived AI errors or unexpected behavior. Investigate these reports promptly to identify root causes (e.g., data issues, model limitations, edge cases) and implement corrective actions (e.g., model retraining, guideline updates).
  • Clinician Engagement: Involve clinical end-users throughout the entire AI lifecycle – from problem definition and data annotation to validation design, workflow integration, and post-deployment feedback. This ensures the developed tools address genuine clinical needs and fosters trust and appropriate use. Siriraj's experience with existing AI projects provides a foundation for this collaborative approach.7

Model Lifecycle Management Strategy

Adopt MLOps (Machine Learning Operations) practices from the outset. This involves using tools and processes for:

  • Reproducibility: Tracking code, data versions, model parameters, and environments to ensure experiments and results can be reproduced.
  • Versioning: Managing different versions of datasets, models, and code.
  • Automated Pipelines: Automating training, validation, and deployment processes where possible.
  • Monitoring: Integrating model performance monitoring into the deployment infrastructure.
  • Collaboration: Facilitating collaboration between data scientists, engineers, and IT operations.

Implementing AI in healthcare is complex and carries inherent risks. A prudent strategy involves a phased approach. Begin with applications that build on existing successes (e.g., expanding imaging AI capabilities beyond chest X-ray 7) or address clear efficiency needs with lower direct clinical decision-making risk (e.g., documentation assistance). Early wins in these areas can build institutional confidence, demonstrate tangible value, and allow the team to refine data governance, validation, integration, and MLOps processes before tackling more complex or higher-risk applications involving autonomous decision support or novel diagnostic capabilities.

X. Conclusion and Strategic Recommendations

Siriraj Hospital is uniquely positioned to leverage locally deployed and fine-tuned artificial intelligence to significantly enhance clinical care, operational efficiency, research, and education. Its status as Thailand's largest public hospital and a major referral center provides access to vast and diverse clinical data. Strategic investments in advanced digital infrastructure, including 5G, hybrid cloud, and high-performance computing, coupled with existing AI initiatives and a dedicated Innovation Lab, demonstrate strong institutional readiness and ambition.1

Local deployment offers critical advantages, particularly in ensuring compliance with Thailand's PDPA by maintaining control over sensitive patient data, enhancing security, and enabling low-latency applications.13 Fine-tuning models like Gemma for Thai medical language and MONAI/TotalSegmentator for specific imaging tasks using Siriraj's own data and protocols is key to maximizing relevance, accuracy, and clinical acceptance.30 High-impact opportunities exist across diagnostics, clinical documentation, treatment planning, patient monitoring, scheduling optimization, and resource management, directly addressing prevalent Thai health issues and Siriraj's operational challenges.5

However, realizing this potential requires navigating significant technical and organizational challenges, including robust data governance, complex IT integration, rigorous validation, and building sustained internal expertise.

Actionable Recommendations for the Data Science Lead:

  1. Prioritize Strategically: Focus initial efforts on a select few high-impact, feasible AI projects. Prioritize applications that address major pain points (e.g., clinician documentation burden using fine-tuned Thai Gemma, scheduling optimization) or build upon existing strengths (e.g., expanding validated imaging AI applications 7). Use a clear prioritization framework considering clinical impact, efficiency gains, feasibility, and strategic alignment.
  2. Establish Robust Data Governance Immediately: Make PDPA compliance 49 and ethical data handling for AI a top priority. Work with legal, ethical, and IT departments to finalize policies for data access, consent management (especially for AI training), rigorous anonymization/de-identification, and data security before large-scale model development using patient data begins. Appoint a DPO if one is not already designated.49 Address data quality and standardization issues proactively.
  3. Build and Foster Internal Capabilities: Invest strategically in developing a skilled, cross-functional internal AI team encompassing data science, ML engineering, data engineering, clinical informatics, and IT integration roles. Leverage the Siriraj Innovation Lab 17 and external partnerships 13 for training and collaboration, but focus on building sustainable in-house expertise. Foster a culture of close collaboration between technical teams and clinical departments.
  4. Develop a Standardized Local Validation Framework: Create clear, rigorous protocols for validating AI models using Siriraj's data before clinical deployment. This framework should include technical performance assessment, clinical utility evaluation (involving end-users), bias/fairness checks, and usability testing. Define processes for ongoing monitoring and managing model updates.
  5. Leverage Infrastructure Synergistically: Maximize the use of existing advanced infrastructure – the 5G private network and MEC for low-latency inference 13, the hybrid cloud for flexible compute and data management 13, and the NVIDIA DGX systems for intensive training.14 Develop a clear strategy for resource allocation and plan for future scalability needs.
  6. Implement MLOps Practices Early: Integrate MLOps principles and tools into the AI development workflow from the beginning to ensure reproducibility, version control, automated testing, deployment monitoring, and efficient lifecycle management for locally deployed models.
  7. Engage Clinicians Continuously: Cultivate strong partnerships with clinical champions across relevant departments. Involve them actively in defining use cases, providing domain expertise, assisting with data annotation/validation, designing workflow integrations, and providing feedback post-deployment. Clinician buy-in and trust are essential for successful adoption.

By pursuing a strategic, well-governed, and collaborative approach focused on local deployment and fine-tuning, Siriraj Hospital can harness the transformative power of AI to solidify its position as a leading medical institution in Thailand and the region, delivering significant benefits to its patients and the broader healthcare system.
